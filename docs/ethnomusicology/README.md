# Musical context, participants, and performance specification

The model represents a living musical practice through a limited set of physical
and computational decisions. It should not erase variation by presenting one
convenient tuning or gesture map as the only correct setar. Equally, respect for
variation is not an excuse for vague engineering: the project must identify the
specimen, player context, intended gesture, and measured outcome precisely.

These chapters define proposed collaboration and evidence practices. They do not
claim that a panel has been recruited or that a tuning collection has been
approved. The primary performer and empirical sources are
R-SETAR-TABASSIAN and R-FRETTING-POORHAYDARI-2023 in the
[research ledger](../research/evidence-ledger.md). Their short source summaries
should not be expanded into unverified historical claims.

Read these alongside the mechanical protocols, especially
[strings and setup](../measurement/strings-frets-and-setup.md),
[contact capture](../measurement/pluck-contact-and-motion.md), and
[independent validation](../measurement/independent-validation.md). A cultural
description cannot replace physical measurement, and a measured frequency table
cannot replace musical context. The two kinds of evidence should be linked rather
than collapsed into one.

## Roles

Players can explain technique, intention, and musical acceptability. Makers can
explain construction and setup choices. Scholars can clarify terminology,
historical sources, and conceptual distinctions. Audio and mechanical researchers
can design measurements and analyze mechanisms. Software developers can make the
representation usable and reliable. Individuals may hold several roles, but no
one role automatically supplies every kind of authority.

Record disagreements as scoped evidence. A player may prefer a behavior that
differs from another player's practice; a maker may describe a construction that
is not observable on the selected specimen. The project should preserve those
distinctions and design tests where appropriate. Consensus is useful when real,
but should not be manufactured by averaging all responses into a single number.

## Product boundary

The plugin's labels, presets, demonstrations, and claims should be reviewed as
carefully as its equations. A designed variant should not be named as an exact
replica of an unmeasured instrument. A consulted player's name should not imply
endorsement without explicit agreement. A traditional melody should not be
treated as a license to distribute someone else's recording or arrangement.

The gate is a clear statement of what the instrument supports, whose input shaped
it, what remains unrepresented, and what permissions apply. This is a practical
engineering requirement: it prevents misleading controls, unusable datasets,
and evaluation criteria that reward the wrong musical behavior.
