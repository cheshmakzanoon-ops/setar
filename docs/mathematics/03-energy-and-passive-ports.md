# Energy bookkeeping and passive mechanical ports

## What passivity does and does not establish

A component is passive with respect to specified ports when its stored energy
cannot grow beyond initial energy plus supplied work. This is a statement about
the complete input-output pairing, not a synonym for “has damping” or “does not
explode in one render.” A physically lossy component can be numerically active
if its force is evaluated at an inconsistent time. Conversely, a passive
approximation can still have unacceptable pitch, decay, or radiation errors.

This chapter takes the semidiscrete system
\[
M\dot{\mathbf v}=-K\mathbf q-C\mathbf v+B_p\mathbf f,\qquad
\dot{\mathbf q}=\mathbf v,
\tag{P1}
\]
with symmetric \(M>0\), \(K\ge0\), \(C\ge0\), and fixed \(B_p\). The energy and
collocated output are
\[
H=\tfrac12\mathbf v^TM\mathbf v+\tfrac12\mathbf q^TK\mathbf q,
\qquad \mathbf v_p=B_p^T\mathbf v.
\tag{P2}
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
\dot H=\mathbf f^T\mathbf v_p-\mathbf v^TC\mathbf v.
\tag{P3}
\]
Thus force and velocity are power conjugates. Displacement output is useful for
observation but is not the instantaneous power-conjugate output of force.

The equations below are derived here for this specific system. The use of
potential-based contact is informed by Bilbao, Torin, and Chatziioannou,
*Numerical Modeling of Collisions in Musical Instruments*,
<https://doi.org/10.3813/AAA.918813>, particularly its energy-based treatment.
That source is not a proof for an arbitrary plugin graph or integration method.

## Mechanical connection of two components

Let a string subsystem and a body subsystem expose velocities
\(\mathbf v_s=B_s^T\mathbf v^{(s)}\) and
\(\mathbf v_b=B_b^T\mathbf v^{(b)}\). Define one internal connection force
\(\mathbf f\), applied to the string as \(B_s\mathbf f\) and to the body as
\(-B_b\mathbf f\). A rigid connection imposes \(\mathbf v_s=\mathbf v_b\).
Its net power is therefore
\(\mathbf f^T(\mathbf v_s-\mathbf v_b)=0\).

This cancellation requires the same force, the same physical point and direction,
and the same time evaluation on both sides. A bridge-force signal sent through a
one-sample delay to a body while the string receives an undelayed reaction is a
different, delayed coupling. It must be analyzed as such; the continuous argument
does not justify it.

For a primary cross-domain comparison, Torin, Hamilton, and Bilbao explicitly
assemble energy across strings, membranes, contact, and air in sections 2.6 and
3.5 of *An Energy Conserving Finite Difference Scheme for the Simulation of
Collisions in Snare Drums*:
<https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2014/dafx14_alberto_torin_an_energy_conserving_fini.pdf>.
The relevant lesson for this project is the complete-system work audit. Its
particular acoustic and membrane discretizations are not adopted here merely
because their combined example is stable.

For a compliant connector with relative displacement
\(\mathbf r=B_s^T\mathbf q_s-B_b^T\mathbf q_b\), introduce a potential \(V_c\)
and, if appropriate, dissipation \(D_c\). The connector force opposing \(\mathbf r\)
is \(-\nabla V_c(\mathbf r)\) plus dissipative terms. Include \(V_c\) in total
stored energy. A spring does not dissipate energy merely because its force opposes
displacement; it stores and returns energy.

An observation-only body filter does not perform this mechanical exchange.
It may be a consciously reduced radiation stage, but it cannot reproduce
bidirectional bridge loading simply by making the output sound resonant.

## Coordinate transforms must preserve work

Suppose a local contact velocity is \(\mathbf v_\ell=R\mathbf v_g\).
The corresponding global force must be \(\mathbf f_g=R^T\mathbf f_\ell\), so
\(\mathbf f_g^T\mathbf v_g=\mathbf f_\ell^T\mathbf v_\ell\).
For a rotation, \(R^{-1}=R^T\), but the transpose rule is more general than
rotation. It also applies to interpolation, modal projection, and constraint maps.

Test this identity with non-axis-aligned normals. An implementation may pass all
axis-aligned tests while using the inverse rather than transpose of a general
map. If a map includes length scaling or multiple coordinates, those operations
are no longer interchangeable.

A port carrying translation and rotation has power
\(\mathbf F^T\mathbf v+\mathbf M^T\boldsymbol\omega\). When moving a wrench
between reference points, include the moment generated by the lever arm. Ignoring
that term changes work for rotating bridge or body motion. The full rigid-body
wrench/twist transform is an extension requiring its own coordinate contract.

## A synchronized discrete reference

Use implicit midpoint for the linear part:
\[
\frac{\mathbf q^{n+1}-\mathbf q^n}{k}
=\overline{\mathbf v},\qquad
M\frac{\mathbf v^{n+1}-\mathbf v^n}{k}
=-K\overline{\mathbf q}-C\overline{\mathbf v}
+B_p\mathbf f^{n+1/2}.
\tag{P4}
\]
Multiplying the second equation by \(k\overline{\mathbf v}^T\), and using the
first, gives exactly in real arithmetic
\[
H^{n+1}-H^n
=k(\mathbf f^{n+1/2})^TB_p^T\overline{\mathbf v}
-k\overline{\mathbf v}^TC\overline{\mathbf v}.
\tag{P5}
\]

For a joint connection, use the same \(\mathbf f^{n+1/2}\) and enforce equality
of the two midpoint port velocities. The internal works then cancel sample by
sample. This is a useful reference even if a later optimized solver uses a different
time representation. Its purpose is a transparent physical baseline, not an
assertion that midpoint is the final production choice.

For nonlinear potential \(V(\mathbf q)\), replace its gradient by a two-point
discrete gradient satisfying
\[
\overline{\nabla}V(\mathbf q^n,\mathbf q^{n+1})^T
(\mathbf q^{n+1}-\mathbf q^n)
=V(\mathbf q^{n+1})-V(\mathbf q^n).
\tag{P6}
\]
Then the same power proof includes exact potential change. Ordinary midpoint
evaluation \(\nabla V(\overline{\mathbf q})\) satisfies P6 for a quadratic
potential, but not generally for a nonlinear potential. Calling ordinary midpoint
“energy conserving” without this qualification is incorrect.

## Work ledger design

Record the following distinct channels in the reference harness:

- Initial kinetic, linear potential, nonlinear potential, and contact energies.
- Work from deliberate excitation forces and imposed boundary motion.
- Work associated with changing geometry, tension, or other model parameters.
- Dissipation from each declared physical loss mechanism.
- Algorithmic dissipation, if the integrator deliberately introduces it.
- Energy introduced or removed by state projection or topology changes.
- Residual energy imbalance attributable to finite precision or incomplete solves.

The accumulated balance residual is
\[
R_E^n=H^n-H^0-W_{\mathrm{external}}^n
-W_{\mathrm{parameter}}^n-W_{\mathrm{projection}}^n
+D_{\mathrm{physical}}^n+D_{\mathrm{algorithmic}}^n.
\tag{P7}
\]
Define signs once. Work is positive into the modeled system; dissipated energy
is positive out. A reset that removes state is not physical damping and must not
be hidden in its channel.

Normalize diagnostic residuals by a robust scale such as
\(\max(H^0,|W_{\mathrm{external}}|+H^0,H_\star)\), where \(H_\star>0\) is an
explicit fixture scale. Do not divide by the instantaneous energy when it decays
toward zero. Report absolute residual as well so normalization cannot conceal a
large physical error.

## Ports with externally prescribed motion

An ideal motion source can supply unlimited energy. If a finger position is
prescribed, its contact force is a reaction, and supplied work includes that force
times source velocity. The source's energy does not need to be represented as
internal storage, but its work must be recorded. Label it an active control input.

For a stationary contact shape but a moving center, the local coordinate depends
explicitly on the center position. The corresponding geometric work can be
derived through the chain rule or a discrete potential difference. Interpolating
velocity at the current contact point alone omits motion of the observation
operator. An unexplained energy increase during a slide may be physical control
work, numerical error, or both; the ledger distinguishes them.

A control smoother changes the time history of source work. It is not an energy
proof. A smoothly varying but energetically inconsistent parameter can inject
energy continuously, and an abrupt but correctly accounted impulse can be a
valid idealized excitation.

## A coupled solve in practical steps

1. Enumerate each subsystem's coordinates and immutable mass/stiffness maps.
2. Express all connection directions in one declared global frame.
3. Construct observation maps, then obtain generalized force maps by transpose.
4. Decide whether the connection is rigid, compliant, unilateral, or prescribed.
5. Write one shared unknown force per independent connection direction.
6. Assemble subsystem updates and connection constraints at the same time level.
7. Eliminate internal linear states only after deriving their response to the
   shared forces; retain the exact signs in the reduced contact system.
8. Solve the reduced system with an explicit convergence criterion.
9. Reconstruct subsystem states, recompute port velocities, and verify power
   cancellation independently from the solver's own residual calculation.
10. Commit the new state only after finiteness and balance checks pass.

If a runtime budget motivates sequential coupling, compare it with this joint
reference over the entire intended parameter envelope. Quantify energy error,
frequency shifts, transient response, and runtime. Do not characterize a
sequential approximation as passive solely because the reference is passive.

## Tests that catch interface failures

Use two masses connected by one spring as the smallest dynamic fixture. Excite
only one mass, then confirm equal and opposite internal impulses and bounded total
energy. Replace one mass with a short string and repeat. Reverse the global axis
and confirm that the physical output transforms consistently while energy is
unchanged. Rotate a two-dimensional port and repeat with coupled polarizations.

For a rigid connection, check relative velocity and total momentum in a free,
lossless two-mass system. A lossless rigid constraint changes neither total
momentum nor total energy when initialized compatibly. An inconsistent initial
velocity may require an impact or projection; do not demand conservation from
an undocumented state correction.

Deliberately substitute the wrong interpolation transpose in a negative test.
The work-identity test must fail. Deliberately delay one force side and verify
that the synchronization test detects the change even if the output remains
bounded. These fault-injection tests establish that diagnostics are sensitive
to the errors they claim to detect.

## Failure gates and limitations

Reject a connection if it cannot name both conjugate variables, if force
normalization changes with grid size, or if energy cancellation relies on an
unexplained gain. Reject a nonlinear solve whose residual tolerance is unrelated
to work error. A solver may return a small coordinate increment while still
leaving a large force imbalance.

No energy clamp belongs in the integrator. Scaling a growing state back to a
target energy hides the problem and changes the sound according to a nonphysical
operation. Runtime fault containment may silence a failed voice, but it must
report the failure and invalidate the render as evidence. The next action is
to test contact potentials with the same work contract.
