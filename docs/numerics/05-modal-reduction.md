# Modal reduction with collocated ports and controlled truncation

## The purpose of a reduced model

Modal reduction can make a physical model affordable by retaining a subspace of
mechanical motion. It is not sample playback and need not abandon physical units.
Its principal risk is that a small set of frequencies can look convincing while
missing contact compliance, spatial response, or bridge loading that depends on
discarded modes.

This chapter defines a passive projection of a linear mechanical system and a
verification process for using it as a string or body component. Inputs are
validated mass, stiffness, damping, and port matrices, plus a stated retained
subspace. Outputs are reduced operators, reconstruction and observation maps,
truncation diagnostics, and an empirical validity envelope.

## Generalized eigenproblem and normalization

For symmetric \(M>0\), \(K\ge0\), solve
\[
K\boldsymbol\phi_j=\omega_j^2M\boldsymbol\phi_j.
\tag{M1}
\]
Choose mass normalization
\[
\boldsymbol\phi_i^TM\boldsymbol\phi_j=\delta_{ij}.
\tag{M2}
\]
The primary LAPACK DSYGV documentation specifies this problem and normalization
for its appropriate problem type:
<https://netlib.org/lapack/explore-html/d1/d39/group__hegv_gadc3e6dc69532c1233818df364b7de912.html>.
Do not feed \(M^{-1}K\) to an ordinary symmetric eigensolver; that matrix need
not be symmetric in Euclidean coordinates. A Cholesky-based similarity transform
or a generalized symmetric solver preserves the intended structure.

Check residuals
\(\|K\phi_j-\omega_j^2M\phi_j\|\), mass orthogonality, and reconstruction
of known analytic modes. Eigenvector sign is arbitrary, and vectors inside a
degenerate eigenspace can rotate. A test that requires one exact eigenvector sign
or basis ordering can fail for a valid result. Compare subspaces and port response
when degeneracy is present.

## Reduced operators and power identity

Collect retained basis vectors in V and approximate \(\mathbf q=V\mathbf a\).
Project with \(V^T\):
\[
M_r\ddot{\mathbf a}+C_r\dot{\mathbf a}+K_r\mathbf a
=B_r\mathbf f,
\]
\[
M_r=V^TMV,\quad C_r=V^TCV,\quad K_r=V^TKV,\quad B_r=V^TB_p.
\tag{M3}
\]
For a mass-normalized eigenbasis, \(M_r=I\) and
\(K_r=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_j^2)\). Damping is diagonal only when its
operator is diagonal in the same basis. Do not discard off-diagonal damping
merely because independent oscillators are convenient.

The port velocity must be
\[
\mathbf v_p=B_r^T\dot{\mathbf a}.
\tag{M4}
\]
Then reduced input work is \(\mathbf f^T\mathbf v_p\). Congruence projection
preserves nonnegativity of M, K, and C on the retained subspace, so the reduced
linear model is passive with these collocated ports. This does not prove that
its impedance is sufficiently accurate.

If an observation at a different point is desired, add a separate output map.
It may not be the power-conjugate output of the input force, and its transfer
function need not satisfy the same positive-real property. Keep mechanical port
and microphone-like observation roles distinct.

## Analytic string modes

For a uniform simply supported string,
\[
\phi_j(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\mu L}}\sin(j\pi x/L)
\]
is mass normalized in the continuous inner product. The corresponding modal
coordinate has units metre times square root kilogram, so
\(\frac12\dot a_j^2\) is energy. Alternatively, use dimensionless sine shapes
and retain modal mass \(\mu L/2\). Both conventions are valid; mixing their
force amplitudes is not.

The modal frequencies are
\[
\omega_j^2=c^2(j\pi/L)^2+\kappa^2(j\pi/L)^4.
\tag{M5}
\]
For the specific spatial-gradient loss law, modal decay parameters are
\[
d_j=\sigma_0+\sigma_1(j\pi/L)^2.
\tag{M6}
\]
A phenomenological fit \(d_j=a+b\omega_j^2\) is a different law, especially for
a stiff string. Do not identify its coefficient b with \(\sigma_1\) without
deriving the mapping under appropriate assumptions.

An uncoupled modal oscillator admits exact sampled propagation for constant
parameters and specified forcing. That exact propagation does not automatically
solve nonlinear contact or bidirectional coupling. A force held constant over a
step has a different exact transition from an impulse or linearly varying force.

## Truncation and contact compliance

A point contact excites a broad spatial spectrum. Retaining only low modes
changes the local compliance and can increase apparent penetration or alter
release timing even when low modal frequencies are exact. The missing modes
are not merely inaudible output; they can influence the force generated by a
nonlinear interaction.

For a linear static point load, the compliance at a collocated port is
\[
Y_{\mathrm{static}}=B_r^TK_r^{-1}B_r
\]
when K is positive definite and static equilibrium exists. Compare retained and
reference compliance. Dynamic mobility adds frequency dependence, damping, and
inertia; a static match alone is insufficient.

A residual-flexibility correction can approximate discarded static compliance,
but it introduces an additional mechanical element that needs passivity and
high-frequency analysis. Do not append an arbitrary gain correction to the
contact displacement. If high modes are replaced by an effective spring or
dashpot, state its derivation and frequency validity range.

## Choosing a subspace

Frequency cutoff is a useful starting point, not a complete selection rule.
Modes with weak radiation can still matter at a contact or bridge port. Conversely,
a mode with negligible controllability from supported excitations and negligible
observability at relevant outputs may have little effect on the intended task.
Analyze both force participation and response participation.

Retaining a basis selected from one boundary configuration can become inadequate
under a distant fret or tuning condition. A fixed full-domain modal basis with
contacts preserves state continuity, but the contact resolution may require more
modes. A configuration-dependent basis reduces local cost at the expense of state
transfer and changing operator work.

For a body model, mode shapes and modal residues carry more information than
frequencies alone. A list of resonances with arbitrary positive amplitudes is
not enough to establish a reciprocal multiport mechanical model. Use common
mode-shape factors across all ports so the residue matrices have the required
structure.

## Initial conditions and projection

For mass-normalized V, the displacement coefficients are
\(\mathbf a_0=V^TM\mathbf q_0\) and velocity coefficients
\(\dot{\mathbf a}_0=V^TM\mathbf v_0\). These are mass-inner-product projections.
Compute discarded kinetic energy explicitly. Displacement projection in the
mass norm does not necessarily minimize strain-energy error.

A triangular displacement profile has a slope jump. For an ideal tension string
its energy can be finite, but adding Euler–Bernoulli stiffness makes the curvature
singular; the continuum bending energy is not finite for the ideal sharp kink.
A finite modal sum is smooth and has finite energy, but that energy need not
converge as the number of modes grows. Use a smooth physically motivated pluck
shape or an applied force/contact model when testing stiff-string energy
convergence.

This distinction is crucial: convergence of waveform displacement in an \(L^2\)
norm does not imply convergence of a higher-derivative energy norm. A benchmark
must name the norm in which its initial condition is admissible.

## Stable reduction is not sufficient accuracy

Compare a reduced model with a high-resolution reference using port impulse
responses, mobility matrices, contact trajectories, and energy exchange.
Measure errors as modes are added. Do not judge reduction only from a final
microphone output, because radiation filtering can hide significant mechanical
errors that later reappear in nonlinear force generation.

Use several excitation locations, including locations near nodes of retained
modes. A model can appear accurate at one point because omitted modes happen
to have small participation there. Include multiple pluck directions and bridge
port directions for a two-polarization or multiport model.

Separate numerical time error from modal truncation by using a sufficiently
accurate time reference for each fixed reduced system. Exact unforced modal
propagation is useful for this separation, but a coupled nonlinear test still
needs a consistent force integrator.

## Preparation and runtime

Perform eigenanalysis, basis selection, normalization checks, and factorization
outside real time. Store immutable reduced matrices, source model identity,
normalization convention, physical units, and supported parameter range. Prepare
modal recurrence coefficients without allocating in the callback.

Avoid blindly vectorizing all modes as independent when damping or contact
couples them. Exploit diagonal free dynamics and low-rank ports only after deriving
the corresponding joint solve. A rank-one contact force can still couple every
retained mode through its shared displacement.

## Rejection gates and next action

Reject a reduced model with inconsistent normalization, noncollocated force and
power output, arbitrary residue signs, or unexplained energy creation under
truncation. Reject an accuracy claim based solely on matched resonance frequencies.
Record the retained subspace and omitted-mode error for each supported operating
configuration.

The next action is to compare this reduction with a spatial finite-element
reference where geometry or boundary complexity makes a uniform grid unsuitable.
Both representations should expose the same mechanical port contract.
