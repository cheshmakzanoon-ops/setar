# Analytic benchmarks, manufactured solutions, and fault injection

## Verification is not calibration

Verification asks whether the implementation solves its stated equations.
Calibration estimates physical parameters from observations. Validation asks
whether the resulting model represents the instrument adequately for a specified
purpose. Keep these activities separate. A calibrated model can hide a numerical
frequency error by adjusting tension, and a verified solver can still use an
inadequate contact law.

This chapter specifies independent fixtures. They are synthetic unless explicitly
identified as measurements elsewhere. Their parameter values must not migrate
into an instrument preset without provenance. A benchmark should state the
continuous problem, discrete variant, initial and boundary data, expected
quantities, comparison norm, and failure criterion.

## Benchmark hierarchy

Start with algebraic identities and elementary mechanics, then spatial operators,
then time integration, then coupling, and finally nonlinear gestures. A full
instrument waveform is a poor first diagnostic because many errors can compensate.

Each fixture should have at least one independent expectation: closed form,
symbolic identity, a different numerical representation, or a tightly converged
reference with its own error study. A golden waveform generated by the production
code is a regression record, not an independent proof.

## Free mass and impulse

For \(M\dot v=BJ\delta(t-t_0)\), momentum jumps by \(BJ\):
\[
v^+=v^-+M^{-1}BJ.
\tag{V1}
\]
The energy increment is
\[
\Delta H=(v^-)^TBJ+
\frac12J^TB^TM^{-1}BJ.
\tag{V2}
\]
Use this fixture to test mass normalization, point-force injection, and impulse
semantics. A digital force sample F represents impulse \(kF\) only under the
specified hold or quadrature rule. Do not confuse a force amplitude with an
impulse parameter.

Test zero impulse, opposite signed impulses, nonzero initial velocity, and a
non-axis-aligned port. The energy increment can be negative when the impulse
opposes existing motion; an impulse is an external work input, not automatically
an energy increase.

## Scalar oscillator

For \(m\ddot q+c_d\dot q+k_sq=0\), define
\(\Omega^2=k_s/m\) and \(d=c_d/(2m)\). In the underdamped case, use the exact
solution from the continuum chapter with arbitrary nonzero initial displacement
and velocity. Also test critical damping and overdamping with stable limiting
formulas.

Compare coordinate, velocity, phase, energy, and decay. An integrator can match
one coordinate at one sample while having a significant velocity error.
For a lossless midpoint test, verify the exact discrete energy and the phase
relation separately. For central differences, compare with its modified energy
and exact discrete amplification polynomial before comparing with the continuum.

Use a forcing case whose exact solution is known or whose convolution integral
is independently evaluated. This catches a solver that propagates free motion
correctly but applies force at the wrong time level.

## Ideal string travel and modal benchmarks

For zero rigidity and no loss, a simply supported string has
\(\Omega_j=cj\pi/L\). Initialize one sine mode and compare displacement and
velocity at several points, including points not aligned with a mode antinode.
Check nodes explicitly; a near-zero observation requires an absolute error scale.

A smooth localized pulse before endpoint reflection can test propagation speed
against the d'Alembert solution. Choose compact or sufficiently localized smooth
initial data and restrict the comparison to a region where the intended
boundary influence is known. Do not compare a free-space formula after a reflected
wave has entered the observation interval.

For the special ideal lossless FDTD case at unit Courant number, the discrete
scheme has a particularly simple propagation structure. It is a useful limiting
test, but its special accuracy does not generalize to stiff or lossy strings.
Production use should still avoid relying on a marginal bound without examining
the actual boundary spectrum and initialization.

## Stiff-string eigenmodes

For simply supported endpoints,
\[
\Omega_j^2=c^2(j\pi/L)^2+\kappa^2(j\pi/L)^4.
\tag{V3}
\]
For the finite-difference spatial operator, replace \((j\pi/L)^2\) with
\[
s_j=\frac4{h^2}\sin^2(j\pi/(2N)).
\tag{V4}
\]
These two expectations answer different questions. V4 verifies the assembled
discrete operator; V3 measures spatial approximation error.

Add the specified damping law and compare exact modal decay. Do not use a
frequency-squared phenomenological damping law as the expected result for a
spatial-gradient damping implementation. The two agree only under additional
approximations that must be stated.

## Static equilibrium checks

For an ideal tension string under a positive point force F at \(x=a\), the
static displacement is piecewise linear:
\[
u(x)=
\begin{cases}
Fx(L-a)/(TL),&x\le a,\\
Fa(L-x)/(TL),&x\ge a.
\end{cases}
\tag{V5}
\]
Use it to test force injection and equilibrium in the tension-only model.
It is not an admissible finite-bending-energy initial field for a stiff string
because of the slope discontinuity.

For a simply supported pure bending model under constant force density \(f_0\),
\[
u(x)=\frac{f_0x(L^3-2Lx^2+x^3)}{24EI}.
\tag{V6}
\]
It satisfies \(EIu_{xxxx}=f_0\), \(u=0\), and \(u_{xx}=0\) at both ends.
Check the center displacement and the relation between static strain energy
and load work. This test catches bending signs and endpoint moments without
involving time integration.

## Manufactured smooth forced solution

Choose
\[
u_\star(x,t)=A\sin(\xi x)e^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega t),
\qquad \xi=j\pi/L,
\tag{V7}
\]
where \(\gamma\) and \(\omega\) are deliberately not constrained to the natural
mode relation. Define the forcing by substitution:
\[
f_\star=\mu\left[
u_{\star,tt}
+(c^2\xi^2+\kappa^2\xi^4)u_\star
+2(\sigma_0+\sigma_1\xi^2)u_{\star,t}\right].
\tag{V8}
\]
The required derivatives are
\[
u_{\star,t}=A\sin(\xi x)e^{-\gamma t}
[-\gamma\cos(\omega t)-\omega\sin(\omega t)],
\]
\[
u_{\star,tt}=A\sin(\xi x)e^{-\gamma t}
[(\gamma^2-\omega^2)\cos(\omega t)+2\gamma\omega\sin(\omega t)].
\]

This fixture checks forcing, damping, stiffness, and time accuracy together.
Use the exact initial displacement and velocity. Evaluate f at the time level
required by the candidate scheme. A manufactured source sampled at the wrong
time can reduce convergence order even if the homogeneous integrator is correct.

To test nonzero boundary work, use a smooth polynomial in x multiplied by a
smooth time function, prescribe the corresponding endpoint displacement and
slope or tractions, and compute the source analytically. Avoid choosing a field
whose endpoint velocity vanishes, because that would not exercise the boundary
power sign.

## Contact and potential fixtures

Test the two-point potential identity independently of dynamics using endpoint
pairs in separation, compression, onset crossing, release crossing, and the
near-equal limit. Compare the optimized divided difference against high-accuracy
quadrature of its integral representation.

For the free-mass contact, solve the scalar monotone residual with a strict
bracketed reference. Verify the production root, force, and energy exchange.
Then attach a linear oscillator and finally a string. Do not begin with a
large distributed contact and infer correctness from the absence of a crash.

For a conservative rebound, total energy is the relevant invariant; kinetic
energy alone decreases during compression because potential energy increases.
Track peak penetration and contact duration as separate convergence observables.
A stable penalty method can still have a poorly resolved collision.

## Coupling and reciprocity fixtures

Use two masses with a rigid or compliant connection, then replace one with a
short string. Check shared-force signs and internal work cancellation. For a
linear multiport structure, compare transfer functions after swapping input and
output ports in conjugate units.

Rotate a two-polarization fixture and confirm covariance. Translate an otherwise
identical geometric description by a large irrelevant world-space offset and
check that local-gap computation remains physically equivalent within its
precision budget.

Test different host block partitions for the same sample-accurate event sequence.
If the physical result changes beyond the declared rounding budget, inspect
event scheduling and mutable configuration snapshots before blaming the
integrator.

## Fault injection

Deliberately invert a force sign, omit an interpolation transpose, change one
boundary coefficient, violate the explicit grid condition, cap Newton too early,
and inject a nonfinite value into a diagnostic build. Each corresponding
diagnostic should fail for the intended reason.

Negative tests are valuable because a passing diagnostic can otherwise be
vacuous. An energy test that never uses nonzero velocity cannot detect an input
power sign error. A contact test that never crosses a grid cell cannot detect
discontinuous support switching.

Keep fault injection isolated from normal production operation. The objective
is to establish test sensitivity, not to expose users to unstable audio.

## Evidence report and rejection gates

For each fixture, report equation variant, units, parameters, discretization,
reference method, measured error, proposed budget, and pass/fail result.
Include the maximum and an appropriate aggregate error; a mean can conceal one
bad contact onset or boundary sample.

Reject claims based on unexecuted commands, fabricated benchmark values, or
expected results generated by the code under test. A source derivation and a
successful build are not substitutes for an actual run.

The primary physical-model references are listed in the mathematical volume.
All analytic fixtures in this chapter are derived by direct substitution or
elementary mechanics, making their provenance inspectable. Continue with
convergence studies and golden-data policy before promoting regression files
to release evidence.
